Workshop guide

Vibe coding is typing a wish, accepting whatever comes out, and hoping. Working with an AI coding agent is different: the AI reads your files and rewrites them, and you are the engineer in charge. You tell it exactly what you want, check everything it does, and keep versions you can go back to. The five workshops are the five habits that make that work.

Say this once at kickoff: the AI is a fast but careless intern, and you are the boss. It does what you say, guesses when you are vague, and is sometimes wrong without telling you.

Run each one as I do, we do, you do: demo it live (4 to 5 min), everyone tries the same thing together (3 min), then they use it on their own project. Use one running example all day, a school recycling tracker, so each demo picks up where the last one ended.

1. Brief the AI

9:20, 10 min
Habit
Give the AI a clear brief before it writes anything, and get a plan before code.
Why
The AI only knows what is in your message and your files. Anything you leave out, it guesses.
Teach
At 9:20, off the computer. A good brief has four parts, on the card: who uses it, what real problem it solves, what it does (2 or 3 things, not 10), how it looks. Start from a sustainability problem you have actually seen. "Stop climate change" is not an app. "Help my family waste less food" is.
Demo
At 9:30, as soon as building opens. First the vibe version: "make a recycling app". Show the generic result. Then the brief: "Students at my school use this to log what they recycled today. Show a running total and a simple chart. Green and white colors." Compare. Then show planning: "Do not build yet. Tell me your plan in three steps." The AI answers in words and changes no code.
Try it
Students write their four-part brief on the card before they type anything.

2. One change, then test

10:00, 10 min
Habit
Ask for one change, test it yourself, then ask for the next.
Why
The AI never runs your app. It writes the code and assumes it works. You are the only tester. If you ask for six things and something breaks, you cannot tell which one did it.
Demo
Add to the tracker: "Add a weekly goal of 100 items and show how close we are. Keep everything else the same." Then test like a user: click every button, type nothing and press Add, refresh the page and check the data is still there. Then ask for six features at once and show how hard it is to find what broke.
Teach
The loop on the card: Ask, Test, Keep or Undo. Say what should not change. Every AI change is saved in History, so each working version is a save point you can return to. Small requests also use less of the AI usage bar.
Try it
Everyone makes one small request, then tests three things: every button, an empty input, a refresh.

3. Debug it properly

10:45, 10 min
Habit
When something breaks, report it like an engineer and find the cause before the fix.
Why
"It's broken, fix it" makes the AI guess, and a wrong guess adds a second bug on top of the first.
Demo
Break the tracker on purpose. Show the three moves in order. One: the yellow error bar and Ask AI to fix this. Two: a real bug report, "When I press Add with an empty box, I expected nothing to happen, but instead it added a blank row." Three: "Explain what is causing this before you change anything."
Teach
The bug report on the card: what I did, what I expected, what happened instead. If two fixes in a row fail, stop. Press Undo or open History, go back to the last version that worked, and ask for it a different way. Piling fix on fix is how apps get wrecked.
Try it
Everyone tries to break their own app on purpose (empty inputs, huge numbers, clicking fast) and writes one bug report.

4. Read what it wrote

11:30, 10 min
Habit
Look at what the AI changed, and do not keep code you cannot explain at all.
Why
It is your app. Judges will ask how it works, and the AI is sometimes wrong in ways you only see by reading.
Demo
Open the Code tab. Every app here is three files: index.html is what is on the page, style.css is how it looks, script.js is what it does. Dots mark the lines the AI changed last. Ask: "Explain what you just changed and why, like I'm new to coding." Then change a heading and the weekly goal by hand and watch the preview update.
Teach
Words, colors and numbers are faster to change by hand and use no AI. The AI sees your current code every time, including your edits, but only the last few chat messages. If something matters, like "keep it green and white", say it again. Check facts too: if the app says a number about water or plastic, look it up, the AI may have made it up.
Try it
Everyone finds one line in script.js, asks the AI what it does, and changes one word, color or number by hand. This is step 4 of the guide on their screen.

5. Check it, then ship it

12:20, 10 min
Habit
Before you ship, test the whole thing from the start as if you had never seen it.
Why
The AI will tell you it is done. Only you can say it is ready.
Demo
Run the final check on the tracker: press Restart and use it start to finish, read every word on the screen, look up any fact or number. Then press Publish and open the link, and press Submit and fill in the title, the problem and how the app solves it.
Teach
Judges score five things: sustainability impact, creativity, does it work, design and ease of use, and presentation. Submit now. You can keep building and submit again until 1:00, and judges see the newest version. From here on, only make small safe changes and test each one.
Presenting
Two minutes at the TV: the problem you noticed, who it helps, a live demo, one thing you would add next. Be ready to point at one part of the code and say what it does.

Prompt card (print one per seat)

Build an app with AI, properly

1. Brief it. Your first message needs 4 things:
  1. Who uses it
  2. What real problem it solves (one you have seen)
  3. What it does (2 or 3 things)
  4. How it looks
Not sure? Ask first: "Do not build yet. Tell me your plan in three steps."
2. One change, then test. Ask → Test → Keep or Undo. Click every button. Try an empty box. Refresh the page.
3. Debug it properly.
"When I ___, I expected ___, but instead ___ happened."
Ask it to explain the cause first. Two fixes failed? Open History, go back to a version that worked, and ask a different way.
4. Read what it wrote. Open the Code tab. Ask: "Explain what you just changed and why." Change small things by hand.
5. Check it, then ship it. Before 1:00: test it start to finish, look up any facts or numbers, press Publish, then Submit.

Roamer script

When a student is stuck, ask before you fix:

  • What did you tell it?
  • What did you expect to happen?
  • What changed since it last worked?

Then help them write a better prompt. That teaches the habit instead of solving one bug.
