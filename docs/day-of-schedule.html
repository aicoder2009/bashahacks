Workshop guide

Vibe coding is typing a wish, accepting whatever comes out, and hoping. Working with an AI coding agent is different: the AI reads your files and rewrites them, and you are the engineer in charge. You tell it exactly what you want, check everything it does, and keep versions you can go back to. The five workshops are the five habits that make that work.

Say this once at kickoff: the AI is a fast but careless intern, and you are the boss. It does what you say, guesses when you are vague, and is sometimes wrong without telling you.

Run each one in three beats: one minute on the habit, a 3 minute live demo, then the Your turn slide stays on the TV while everyone does it on their own app (5 minutes or more). Once that slide is up, stop talking and walk the room. Each Your turn slide ends with a "Done when" line, so a roamer can ask any student to show it. Use one running example all day, a school recycling tracker, so each demo picks up where the last one ended.

1. Brief the AI

9:20, 10 min
Habit
Give the AI a clear brief before it writes anything, and get a plan before code.
Why
The AI only knows what is in your message and your files. Anything you leave out, it guesses.
Teach
At 9:20, off the computer. A good brief has four parts, on the card: who uses it, what real problem it solves, what it does (2 or 3 things, not 10), how it looks. Part three is the app itself, and every app is three things: what you see, what you can do, and what it remembers. Start from a sustainability problem you have actually seen. "Stop climate change" is not an app. "Help my family waste less food" is.
Demo
At 9:30, as soon as building opens. First the vibe version: "make a recycling app". Show the generic result. Then the brief: "Students at my school use this to log what they recycled today. Show a running total and a simple chart. Green and white colors." Compare. Five minutes at most. Then put up the Send your brief slide and show planning once: "Do not build yet. Tell me your plan in three steps." The AI answers in words and changes no code. Point out that the AI builds a small version 1 with one main feature and says what it left out. That list is their next changes.
Your turn
9:24 to 9:30, pen and card. Students write their four-part brief. For "what it does" they write what you see, what you do, and what it remembers. Then they swap cards at 9:28 and circle anything their neighbor left vague. Done when a neighbor can say back what the app does. After the 9:30 demo they type the brief and send it, or ask for a plan first.

2. One change, then test

10:00, 10 min
Habit
Ask for one change, test it yourself, then ask for the next.
Why
The AI never runs your app. It writes the code and assumes it works. You are the only tester. If you ask for six things and something breaks, you cannot tell which one did it.
Demo
Add to the tracker: "Add a weekly goal of 100 items and show how close we are. Keep everything else the same." Then test like a user: ask the room what to try to break, then do it. Click every button, type nothing and press Add, press Restart and check the data is still there. Only if there is time: ask for six features at once and show how hard it is to find what broke.
Teach
Build in this order: make the one main thing work, check it remembers after Restart, add the next feature one at a time, make it look good last. Then the loop on the card: Ask, Test, Keep or Undo. Say what should not change. Every AI change is saved in History, so each working version is a save point you can return to. Small requests also use less of the AI usage bar.
Your turn
10:04 to 10:10, on their own app. Ask for the next step only and add "Keep everything else the same." Then three tests: click every button, leave a box empty and press the button, press Restart and check what they saved is still there. Done when all three tests pass, or they pressed Undo.

3. Debug it properly

10:45, 10 min
Habit
When something breaks, report it like an engineer and find the cause before the fix.
Why
"It's broken, fix it" makes the AI guess, and a wrong guess adds a second bug on top of the first.
Demo
Break the tracker on purpose before the workshop, so there is a real bug on the screen. Show the three moves in order, and have the room fill in the blanks of the bug report before you type it. One: the yellow error bar. Read the message out loud, then press Ask AI to fix this. Two: a real bug report, "When I press Add with an empty box, I expected nothing to happen, but instead it added a blank row." Three: "Explain what is causing this before you change anything."
Teach
The bug report on the card: what I did, what I expected, what happened instead. If two fixes in a row fail, stop. Press Undo or open History, go back to the last version that worked, and ask for it a different way. Piling fix on fix is how apps get wrecked.
Your turn
10:49 to 10:55. Three tests, each with its usual cause on the slide. Press the button with an empty box (a blank item means nothing checks the box). Type a word where a number goes (NaN means Not a Number: it did math on a word). Press Restart (data gone means it was never saved). Found one? Send the bug report from the card and ask for the cause first. No bug yet? Swap seats and try to break a neighbor’s. Done when they have sent one bug report and read the cause.

4. Read what it wrote

11:30, 10 min
Habit
Look at what the AI changed, and do not keep code you cannot explain at all.
Why
It is your app. Judges will ask how it works, and the AI is sometimes wrong in ways you only see by reading.
Demo
Open the Code tab. Every app here is three files: index.html is what is on the page, style.css is how it looks, script.js is what it does. Dots mark the lines the AI changed last. Ask: "Explain what you just changed and why, like I'm new to coding." Then change a heading and the weekly goal by hand and watch the preview update. Ask the room which file each change lives in before you open it. Then open script.js and find four things with the room: a comment, a variable, an event (what runs when you press a button), and where it saves with localStorage.
Teach
Words, colors and numbers are faster to change by hand and use no AI. The AI sees your current code every time, including your edits, but only the last few chat messages. If something matters, like "keep it green and white", say it again. Check facts too: if the app says a number about water or plastic, look it up, the AI may have made it up.
Your turn
11:34 to 11:40. Open the Code tab. Change one word in index.html and check the Preview. Change one color in style.css. In script.js, find a comment, a variable, and where it saves. Pick one line they do not get and ask the AI what it does. Done when they can point at one line and say what it does. This is step 4 of the guide on their screen.

5. Check it, then ship it

12:20, 10 min
Habit
Before you ship, test the whole thing from the start as if you had never seen it.
Why
The AI will tell you it is done. Only you can say it is ready.
Demo
Two minutes at most on the tracker: press Restart, use it start to finish, then press Publish and open the link. Do not submit the Demo team.
Teach
Judges score five things: sustainability impact, creativity, does it work, design and ease of use, and presentation. Submit now. You can keep building and submit again until 1:00, and judges see the newest version. From here on, only make small safe changes and test each one.
Your turn
12:21 to 12:30, every team. Press Restart and use the app start to finish. Look up every fact and number. Press Publish and open the link. Press Submit and fill in the title, the problem and how the app solves it. Done when the screen says Submitted. After that, the neighbor test: hand the app to a neighbor, say nothing, fix where they get stuck, and submit again.
Presenting
Two minutes at the TV: the problem you noticed, who it helps, a live demo, one thing you would add next. Be ready to point at one part of the code and say what it does.

Prompt card (print one per seat)

Build an app with AI, properly

1. Brief it. Your first message needs 4 things:
  1. Who uses it
  2. What real problem it solves (one you have seen)
  3. What it does (2 or 3 things)
  4. How it looks
Not sure? Ask first: "Do not build yet. Tell me your plan in three steps."
2. One change, then test. Ask → Test → Keep or Undo. Click every button. Try an empty box. Refresh the page.
3. Debug it properly.
"When I ___, I expected ___, but instead ___ happened."
Ask it to explain the cause first. Two fixes failed? Open History, go back to a version that worked, and ask a different way.
4. Read what it wrote. Open the Code tab. Ask: "Explain what you just changed and why." Change small things by hand.
5. Check it, then ship it. Before 1:00: test it start to finish, look up any facts or numbers, press Publish, then Submit.

Roamer script

During a workshop: when the Your turn slide goes up, walk the room. Ask each team to show you the "Done when" line on the TV.

When a student is stuck, ask before you fix:

  • What did you tell it?
  • What did you expect to happen?
  • What changed since it last worked?

Then help them write a better prompt. That teaches the habit instead of solving one bug.

Out of AI? Teams tab, Add AI. Closed the tab or switched computers? On the Check in page, "Already joined? Get back in with your team code". Lost codes are in the Teams tab.
